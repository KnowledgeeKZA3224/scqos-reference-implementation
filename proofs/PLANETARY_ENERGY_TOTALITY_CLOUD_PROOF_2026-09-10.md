## What the numbers say

The International Energy Agency reports about **28,200 TWh of global electricity consumption in 2025**. Spread across the year, that is about **3.219 TW of average electrical power**.

The U.S. Department of Energy gives a reference figure of roughly **173,000 TW of solar power reaching Earth's surface at a given moment**.

Using those two public numbers:

- the quoted solar flow is about **53,740 times larger** than average world electricity demand;
- average world electricity demand is about **0.0018608%** of that quoted solar flow before real-world losses;
- at an idealized 20% conversion rate, about **0.009304%** of that flow would equal average world demand before storage, transmission, weather, land-use, and reliability losses.

The point is not that solar alone automatically solves the grid. The point is deeper: **the planet is not short of raw incoming energy at the scale of today's electricity demand. The hard problem is coordinating capture, storage, movement, timing, resilience, and release.**
